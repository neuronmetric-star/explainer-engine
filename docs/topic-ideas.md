# Topic ideas: "X vs Y" (Nov 2026)

Our two hits so far are both **Doug vs Gary** comparisons (motivation vs discipline, action taker vs perfectionist).
These keep that shape: one character does the "obvious" thing, the other does the boring thing, and a real study or mechanism
explains who wins. Ranked by **fit × timeliness × strength of the evidence**. Facts below are starting points; each gets full
`sources.md` research once Brian picks it.

**What's trending (Nov 2026):** "locking in" / "winter arc" (discipline before January), money stress and "loud budgeting",
"things actually worth the money", buy-now-pay-later regret, overthinking jokes.
Refinery29 on locking in: https://www.refinery29.com/en-au/locking-in-tiktok-trend-personal-development ·
Ramdam Nov 2026 trends: https://www.ramd.am/blog/trends-tiktok · Start Doing Well (money stress, loud budgeting): https://www.startdoingwell.com/newsletter/tiktok-made-me-do-it-and-i-m-saving-money

| # | Matchup | Hook question | Payoff (what the viewer learns) | Proof / mechanism | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Card vs cash** | Why the heck do you spend more when you tap a card? | Paying with cash *hurts* a little (the "pain of paying"); a card numbs it, so you bid more | MIT study (Prelec & Simester 2001): people bid ~$61 for Celtics tickets with a card vs ~$29 with cash, about double. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-821-listening-to-the-customer-fall-2002/5cc52570803b6e1bbea64585acdce779_redblack20copy.pdf · https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_of_paying | holiday shopping, loud budgeting |
| 2 | **Buy now, pay later vs saving up** | Why the heck does "four easy payments" cost so much? | Splitting a price makes it *feel* smaller, so you buy more and stack payments you forget | Fed data: ~1 in 4 BNPL users paid late; 32% of 18–29 year-olds. https://www.fool.com/money/research/buy-now-pay-later-statistics · https://federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-august-consumer-community-context.htm | holiday season, BNPL regret trend |
| 3 | **The all-nighter vs the sleeper** ("grind" vs sleep) | Why the heck does the guy who sleeps beat the guy who pulls an all-nighter? | After ~17–19 hours awake your brain tests like someone who's had a couple of drinks | Williamson & Feyer 2000: 17–19 h awake ≈ 0.05% blood alcohol on most tests. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1739867 | exams/finals in December, "locking in" |
| 4 | **Looking rich vs being wealthy** | Why the heck is the guy with the nice car the broke one? | Wealth is the money you *don't* spend, so you can't see it; the car is money that's gone | Morgan Housel, *The Psychology of Money* ("wealth is what you don't see"); simple math, no advice | "worth the money" trend, money stress |
| 5 | **Busy vs productive** | Why the heck does the guy doing ten things get nothing done? | Every switch leaves part of your attention stuck on the last task | "Attention residue" (Sophie Leroy, 2009) + APA on task-switching costs (to research) | locking in, New Year planning |
| 6 | **Goals vs systems** (winter arc) | Why the heck do New Year's goals die in two weeks? | A goal is a finish line; a system is what you do daily, and only the system shows up on a rainy Tuesday | Strava "Quitter's Day" (already sourced in motivation-vs-discipline), goal research (Locke & Latham) to balance it | January is weeks away. *Overlaps motivation-vs-discipline: do it as a part 2* |
| 7 | **Overthinker vs doer** | Why the heck does thinking about it more make it worse? | Going over the same worry doesn't solve it; it just replays it (rumination) | Rumination research (Nolen-Hoeksema), to research | "Paid me no mind" overthinking trend. *Overlaps action-vs-perfect* |
| 8 | **Renting vs buying a home** | Who the heck actually wins, the renter or the buyer? | Neither by default: it depends on how long you stay, because buying has big one-time costs | explain costs only, **no personal advice** (PLAYBOOK §1.6) | always searched, housing costs |

**My pick:** #1 (card vs cash) or #3 (all-nighter vs sleeper). Both have one strong, easy-to-picture study, a clear Doug-vs-Gary
contest (an auction; an exam), and a twist the viewer can feel in their own life. #2 pairs with #1 as a two-part money series.
