## 1. Ray: how an 8-minute story is built

### The skeleton (with real timestamps)
| Beat | Ray does | Example (time) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open (one line)** | The whole absurd outcome in one sentence | "So this woman is about to lose everything over a pack of nuts." (0:00) · "So this tiktoker is in big trouble." (0:00) · "Do you remember when a bunch of billionaires died and the whole internet celebrated?" (0:00) |
| **Character card** | Name, age, place, one defining trait, in 10–15 s | "Now, her name is Heather, and Heather is about 40… living in Seoul… She's the executive vice president of Korean Air… her grandpa founded the company… So she's a Nepo." (0:05–0:29) |
| **Desire or flaw** | What they want, and why that's a problem | "big fancy lifestyles cost money. And these two, I guess they just really don't wanna work for that money." (0:29) |
| **"So one day in [year]…"** | The inciting decision, with a date | "So one day in 2024, they come up with a scheme…" (0:40) |
| **The mechanism, step by step** | "So here's what they do. First…" Concrete actions you can picture | the receipt, the moved security tag, the alarm, the bag check, the walk-out (1:01–2:48) |
| **Apparent success, with a number** | "And this scheme works perfectly." Then the money | "$1,000 in 10–15 minutes", then "it works every single time" (2:48–3:28) |
| **Escalation montage** | Repeat with a bigger number each time | "Colorado: 8 stores in 2 days, $32,000. Utah… $21,000. Minnesota… $23,000." (3:51–4:16) |
| **"Now here's the problem…"** | The observer appears (someone who notices the pattern) | "Lululemon has their own internal investigations team… the Lululemon police." (4:35–5:15) · "Particularly this guy, David." (2:35, OceanGate) |
| **Near-miss** | They almost get caught and slip away. The narrator is in disbelief | "I can't believe these guys keep getting away." (6:23) |
| **False ending → "until"** | Fake wrap-up, pause, flip | "And that's the end of all that, right? Right? Wrong." (4:15, Nut Rage) · "they're never gonna get caught… until… until Jay Icon and Apple get sloppy." (6:27–6:36) · "that is definitely the end of this scandal… until," (8:17) |
| **Callback payoff** | Brings back a detail planted minutes earlier | "Remember how I said intentionally diverting a commercial aircraft… is a breach of South Korean aviation law? Well, it is." (8:20) · "But remember, police had already been notified…" (7:26) |
| **"Bam," then the evidence** | The arrest or consequence lands with a sound word, then proof on screen | "And so police show up there and Bam, they arrest them. Here's Jay Icon's mugshot." (7:53) |
| **The aftermath plus a one-line verdict** | The sentence, the numbers, a dry closing line. No CTA | "she's only in there for 143 days." · "he has filed for divorce. So good for him." · "Damn, these two are shameless." |

### Ray's moves
- **2–3 "until" flips per video**, roughly every 2–3 minutes, each after a fake "and that's the end."
- **Plant and pay off.** The rule mentioned at minute 6 is the thing that sinks them at minute 8.
- **Numbers on every beat:** ages, years, dollar amounts, counts ("12 suitcases… $50,000").
- **Honesty asides that build trust:**
  - "but I actually looked into it, and it's apparently not uncommon to use Logitech controllers…"
  - "I'm speculating here."
  - "allegedly"
  - "she hasn't been convicted"
  - "I didn't make these texts up. These are word for word."
- **Sarcastic one-liners after a dumb move:**
  - "I guess they really did not like being questioned."
  - "Oh, boy."
  - "Yeah, that must be it."
  - "I guess these billionaires are just built different."
- **Imagined-visual jokes:** "I imagine it's like the Lululemon police. Like I imagine they look like this."
  **Perfect for our art.** The narrator imagines it, and we cut to a silly prop.
- **Connectors keep it rolling:**
  - "So…" and "And so…" chain every beat.
  - "Now," opens a new beat.
  - "Anyway," returns from an aside.
  - "Meanwhile," cross-cuts to the person who doesn't know (Aaron posting anniversary photos while the hit is being planned).
- **Sound words as punctuation:** "boom," "BAM," "beep, beep, beep."
- **Pace:** roughly 150–160 words per minute, relaxed and conversational. About 1,250–1,400 words per 8 minutes.

## 2. Xevi: the personality

### Signature habits
| Habit | Examples |
|---|---|
| **"See, ___ had a problem."** as the opener and to re-enter a story | "See, MC Hammer had a problem." · "See Curtis '50 Cent' Jackson had a problem." · "Colton had a problem: he was broke and everyone knew it." |
| **Acts out every character** in rapid, invented back-and-forth (paraphrase, clearly playful) | "They said 'Colton.' He said 'huh.' They said 'you broke and everybody knows it.'" · the producer: "you gonna end up just like MC Serch." Redman: "what happened to MC Serch?" Producer: "don't worry, you finna find out." |
| **Comic timing inside the dialogue** | Harley defending Colton: "he may not have the same drip as us… what was I saying?" "You was telling us leave him alone." · The chief: "not you. Everybody else, let's go." |
| **Euphemism dictionary** (funny, and platform-safe) | steal = "forever borrow" · prison = "timeout" / "timeout house" · police = "the popo" · drinking = "sipping" · stabbed = "poked with something not nice" · punch = "yams him in his noodle" · gang = "the blue boys" · gun = left unsaid (a pause) |
| **Running gag word as a callback** | "forever borrowing" comes back 4 times, ending with "I'm telling you I'm forever borrowing, bro." |
| **Talks straight to the viewer** | "you remember MC Hammer, right? the guy who sings Can't Touch This, parachute pants, all that" · "now what you're thinking: who is MC Serch? I'mma tell you" · "I forgot to tell you…" · "listen closely" · "you may be asking yourself how is this possible" |
| **Self-correction gag** | "and actually this is the wrong plane, this was the first plane he borrowed, the one I showed you was like the sixth one. Anyway" |
| **The hinge phrase** | "here's where it starts to get wicked" · "but it all comes to a head when…" |
| **Sound effects in the voice** | "vroom vroom," "kaboom," "ah ah ah" |
| **Understated final quote** | MC Hammer: "I'm just MC Hammer. I have no idea what you may or may not be talking about." · "the rest is history." · "decides to live his life quietly and privately." |

### Voice register
- Casual and playful, with Gen-Z/hip-hop slang ("finna," "drip," "glazing," "cap," "you feel me").
- Short clauses with no setup, fast cuts between speakers.
- Warm toward the underdog, amused at the powerful.
- Roughly 170–185 words per minute.

**How we use it:** take the *personality* (acting out characters, euphemisms, asides, callbacks,
running gags, the deadpan last quote), not an imitation of anyone's dialect. Slang should fit the
voice we cast and stay natural. Never a caricature.

## 3. The merged recipe for our long-form videos

**Opening (0:00–0:15):**
- The Coon Knows question: "What the heck happened to… / How the heck did…" (always "heck")
- Then Ray's cold-open line, delivered Xevi-style: "See, Doug had a problem."

**Body: Ray's skeleton.**
1. Character card.
2. Desire.
3. "So one day in…"
4. Plan mechanics.
5. Success with numbers.
6. Escalation montage.
7. Observer: "now here's the problem."
8. Near-miss.
9. **"until"** (×2–3).
10. Callback.
11. "Bam" + evidence.
12. Aftermath + verdict.

**Delivery: Xevi's voice.**
- The narrator *performs* both sides of every exchange while the silent 3D characters gesture in sync.
- A euphemism dictionary per topic, plus one running-gag word.
- Direct asides ("you're probably wondering…"), honest checks ("I looked into it…"), a
  self-correction gag, imagined-visual jokes ("I imagine it looks like this"), and a deadpan last quote.
