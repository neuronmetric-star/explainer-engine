## Story lessons (add to what we already do)
- **The build-break-fix loop works at any topic** (storm drain, hay hole): "But [new threat]… So you [fix]." Twist every 5–12 s.
- **A number mismatch is a great twist** (31 days of food vs 400 marks; 1 cent vs 3.7 cents to make).
- **The criminal/fool's own mistake is the share moment** ("LOL just kidding"). For us: the detail Doug causes himself.
- **End on a practical check the viewer can do** (spoon scrape, "look at your window next flight") → saves.
- **Deadpan real quote or one-word verdict as the last line** ("Good." / "I just memorize the words.") = our dry last line.
- We do NOT copy graphic true-crime content (#2); structure only. Ad-safe, no violence on screen.

## Visual lessons
1. **Cutaways win.** 4 of the 7 studied videos (storm drain, hay hole, cutting board, oubliette shaft) show the hidden inside and
   the outside at the same time. Every explainer should have one cross-section painting as its "diagram plate".
2. **One bright color = the invisible thing.** Pink chicken juice, white water arrows, yellow mean line, red distance lines.
   The color is kept for that one thing all video long.
3. **The same set keeps growing.** The hay hole becomes a box, then a tunnel network, on ONE background. Escalation by adding
   props to one plate reads better (and costs fewer paintings) than a new background for every beat.
4. **Numbers become objects** (diamond stacks, a counter ticking to 400, milk jugs). Never just a number on screen if it can be a pile.
5. **Faster picture changes:** 1.5–2 s in the top videos (our rule says 2–3 s).
6. **A fixed series banner** at the top ("HIDDEN HOME DANGERS", "The Greatest Ever") tells a scroller what kind of video this is.
7. **Real proof photos** in true stories (the real patio, the real Nigel) make it feel true. Use only images we may use.
8. **Style isn't the reason they win.** Low-poly 3D, photoreal CGI, flat vector and photos all hit millions. Clarity of the
   mechanism and pace are the reason. Our painted 2D + 3D cast is fine and is our brand; keep it consistent.
