## @sketchedpsychology: "Psychology of People Who Can't Stand a Dirty Room"

- Stats: 246.3K plays · 6.5K likes · 3.5K shares · 1.9K saves · 168 comments · posted 2026-11-06
- **Length:** 118 s

**Hook (first 3 s)** `[Hook]`
- Picture: hand-drawn sketch of a person noticing one cushion out of place.
- Words: "Some people cannot walk past a messy room."
- An identity call-out statement aimed at a search phrase ("why can't I stand messy rooms").

**Structure / open loops** `[Script]`
1. Observation.
2. "It goes deeper than that."
3. An explicit "don't scroll" retention ask (~0:19).
4. Debunk ("not fussy").
5. Science: cognitive load.
6. Twist: childhood unpredictability → order = safety.
7. Validation: "You were never fussy…"

**Pacing** `[Visual]`
- New drawing every 1.5–3 s, about 20–40 per minute (est.).

**Visual style** `[Visual]`
- Black-and-white sketch line art on a dark slate-blue textured background.

**Characters** `[Character emotion]`
- Simple sketch people. The emotion is the viewer's own: it reads as being *about you*.

**Captions** `[Visual]`
- Not detailed in the analysis.

**Ending / CTA** `[Growth]`
- A spoken comment prompt: "Does a messy room affect your mood?"

**Steal this** `[Script]`
- **Rule:** end on validation of the viewer ("You were never X, your brain just…"). It drove shares: 3.5K shares on 246K plays is a strong share rate.

## General: lane rules from this batch
- `[Hook]` "Kill the obvious answer" again ("not flexing", "not just tidiness"). Same move as @cardboard_cinema.
- `[Script]` Validation endings ("you were never fussy") and moral endings ("wrong direction") drive shares in general education.
- `[Visual]` Black-background line art (@nolledge, @sketchedpsychology) is cheap to make and readable.
- `[Character emotion]` Physical overreaction to a misunderstood fact is a reusable gag for a silent cast.
- `[Scout]` 2 of 4 accounts lean on borrowed IP (Mameshiba, Tom & Jerry). Exclude borrowed-IP accounts from future scouting.
- `[Growth]` Only one video asked for comments out loud, and it had the most comments (168).
