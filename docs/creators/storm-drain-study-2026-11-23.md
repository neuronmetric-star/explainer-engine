## Five lessons for our scripts
1. **"But… So you…" is the engine.** Every beat: a new problem ("But then a storm hits…"), then the fix ("So you add…").
   A new twist about every 10–12 s, a picture change every 2–3 s. People stay to see what breaks next.
2. **Show it, then name it.** The thing works on screen first; the term comes after: "This is a catch basin."
   (Our §1.6a rule, done in the best order.)
3. **"You" builds it.** Second person puts the viewer in charge ("If you cut a hole in your street…"). For us: Doug builds,
   the narrator talks to the viewer as if they'd do the same.
4. **One silly everyday object to follow.** The tennis ball is the joke and the thing the eye tracks (our "track one object").
5. **The ending echoes the opening and lands on something everyone has seen.** Opens "the rain has somewhere to go", closes
   "let it rain"; the reveal is the manhole you walk past daily. Short, dry, no moral.

## Also noted
- Hook is a "what if", not a question, and the action starts in second 0. We keep our "Why the heck…" question (search phrase),
  but the build should start in the very next sentence.
- Captions 1–3 words per pop, white/yellow with dark stroke (same as ours). Cross-section cutaways read instantly:
  worth a cutaway painting when the mechanism is hidden inside something (a wall, a pipe, a window).
