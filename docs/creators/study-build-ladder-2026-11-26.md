## What the lane is
A 62–78 s narrated build: start with the simplest version of the thing ("If you stack three bones in a row, you have a finger"), then repeat
**"But [problem] → so you add [fix]"** 8–14 times, ending on a named reveal ("Congrats, you just built a throat."). Each fix is named the first
time it appears ("This is a tendon"). Same skeleton as our `script` skill rule 1 ("Perfect!… Except…"), at much higher density.

## Transcript patterns (what to take as technique)
1. **Hook = "If you [simple action], you have a [target]."** One sentence, 2–3 s, then the first "But" in the next sentence. Compare ours: "Why do we yawn?".
2. **The ladder never rests:** the pattern "But X… / So you Y…" is every 3–5 s. 14 rungs in the finger video. Ours has 5–7 per skill rule 1; this lane runs denser.
3. **Name the part at the moment it appears** ("This is a tendon", "This is the overhang"). Gives a caption-worthy gold word per rung.
4. **Body-check lines:** "touch the front of your neck, feel the ridges", "Try to swallow and breathe at once. You cannot." Viewer does something. Good fit for our DIY last line.
5. **Surprise facts as one-line payoffs mid-ladder:** "There is no muscle inside your finger at all." "That crack when you pull a knuckle is not bone." "Astronauts grow up to 5 cm in space."
6. **Round numbers with a yardstick:** "each one weighs as much as the door on a passenger jet"; "nearly 102 degrees, about 3 degrees hotter than a person".
7. **Ending = a named build reveal** ("Congrats, you have built a finger.") and, in one video, "Comment woof. And send this to someone keeping their dog in a metal box." That is a send-to-a-friend close, matching our growth CTA.
8. **Visual (from the topic lists; not watched frame by frame):** 3D / animated assemblies (`#3danimation`). We keep our painted plates + silent cast; take the "part appears when it is named" idea only.
9. **Title formula:** "How a [simple thing] Becomes Your [body part / object]". Searchable, curiosity gap, one line.
