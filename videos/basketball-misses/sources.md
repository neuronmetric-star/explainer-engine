# Sources: Why the heck does the guy who misses the most make the most shots? (basketball version)

**Fable.** Doug, Gary and the one-minute shooting contest are fictional (labelled in the script header).
Gary's numbers (30 shots, 12 made, 18 missed) are invented for the story and add up.

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| "In a Nike ad, Michael Jordan... says he missed more than nine thousand shots." | Nike "Failure" TV ad (1997), Jordan on camera: "I've missed more than 9,000 shots in my career... I've failed over and over and over again in my life. And that is why I succeed." Copy written by Jamie Barrett (Wieden+Kennedy). Shmoop: https://www.shmoop.com/quotes/failed-why-i-succeed.html · WARC, Nike "9,000 Shots": https://www.warc.com · Sneaker Freaker, "Michael Jordan's Legendary 'Failure' Ad": https://www.sneakerfreaker.com. We say "in a Nike ad... says", not that it is a stat sheet number. |
| "Maybe the best player ever" | Opinion, framed as "maybe". |
| Each miss tells him something (short → shoot harder → long → in between) | "Productive failure" (Manu Kapur): learners who try first, fail and then adjust outperform those who wait for instruction. Kapur, Saba & Roll (2023), *npj Science of Learning*: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-023-00165-y.pdf · Imperial College interview: https://blogs.imperial.ac.uk/education-research/?p=374 |
| Caveat | Missing helps only when you adjust from it ("When productive failure fails", Kapur et al.). The script shows Gary adjusting after every miss. |
