# Sources: How ten pages a day beat a whole book in one night

**Doug, Gary, the twelve-book goal, the bookmark and the day counts are fictional** (a reconstruction to show the idea).
The habit research, reading speed and page math are real.

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| "Twelve books in January… that's two hours a day": 12 books × ~300 pages = 3,600 pages ÷ 31 days ≈ 116 pages a day. A printed page holds ~250–300 words; adults read ~238 words/min (non-fiction) to 260 (fiction), so ≈ 1 min per page → ≈ 2 hours a day (**our calculation**) | Brysbaert (2019), "How many words do we read per minute? A review and meta-analysis of reading rate," *Journal of Memory and Language*: https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/linguistics/2019-brysbaert.pdf · BPS Research Digest summary: https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/most-comprehensive-review-date-finds-average-persons-reading-speed-slower |
| "Ten pages a day… about ten minutes": 10 pages × ~1 min/page (**our calculation**, same sources) | Brysbaert 2019 (above); words per page: Kindlepreneur, "Words per page": https://kindlepreneur.com/words-per-page/ · Reedsy, "How many words in a book" (300 words/page rule of thumb): https://reedsy.com/blog/how-many-words-in-a-book/ |
| "Night one, Doug reads a whole book": a ~300-page book ≈ 5 hours at ~1 min/page (**our calculation**; fictional event) | same |
| "Same time and place… it becomes a habit: autopilot": habits form by repeating a behavior in response to the same daily cue until it becomes automatic | Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010), "How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world," *European Journal of Social Psychology* 40:998–1009: https://core.ac.uk/download/70653523.pdf · UCL news: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit |
| "In one study, that took about sixty-six days": in Lally et al., automaticity plateaued after 66 days on average (range 18–254 days; 96 participants, a daily behavior tied to a cue) | Lally et al. 2010 (above) · BPS Research Digest: https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit · UCL Behaviour Change blog, "Busting the 21 days habit formation myth": https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/bsh/2012/06/29/busting-the-21-days-habit-formation-myth/ |
| "Two hours is a fight… ten minutes is no fight": Lally et al. found more complex behaviors took longer to become automatic (simple daily acts like drinking water formed faster than 50 sit-ups). The "fight" wording is the narrator's simplification | Lally et al. 2010 (above) |
| "Gary never skipped" / missing a day: in the same study, missing one day did not significantly hurt habit formation, but very inconsistent people didn't form habits (not stated in the script; background for why Gary's small goal works) | Lally et al. 2010 (above) |
| "Ten pages a day for a year? About twelve books": 10 × 365 = 3,650 pages ÷ ~300 pages per book ≈ 12 books (**our calculation**; book lengths vary) | Reedsy (above) |
| Mid-January quitting (background for Doug quitting on day ten; not stated in the script): Strava's "Quitter's Day," the second Tuesday of January, when its users most often drop New Year's fitness goals | BikeRadar: https://www.bikeradar.com/advice/fitness-and-training/if-youve-kept-your-new-years-resolutions-by-the-end-of-today-youre-officially-winning · Marie Claire UK: https://www.marieclaire.co.uk/opinion/quitters-day-new-years-resolutions |
