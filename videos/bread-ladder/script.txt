[#hook] How does bread rise? [#cracker] Mix flour and water, bake it, and you don't get bread. You get a flat cracker.
[#doug] That's what Doug just made. [#yeast] So you add yeast. It's a tiny living fungus that eats the sugar in flour, and burps out gas. [#soda] The same gas that's in soda.
[#leak] But the gas just leaks out, like air from a loose balloon. [#knead] So you knead the dough. Pushing and folding builds {*gluten,|gluten,} a stretchy net made from the proteins in flour.
[#net] The net traps each burp, like the skin of a balloon. [#stiff] But a fresh net is stiff, so you let it rest somewhere warm. [#rise] The yeast eats faster, the gas piles up, and the net stretches. The dough grows.
[#raw] So, is it bread yet? No. It's still raw dough. [#oven] So you bake it. Heat makes the trapped gas expand, and water inside turns to steam and expands too. The loaf jumps up in the oven.
[#kill] Then the heat kills the yeast, and the net sets, just like an egg white cooks. [#holes] That's why the holes stay.
[#flip] Here's the twist. The yeast that built your bread died in the oven. [#end] So Doug's cracker became bread, thanks to a fungus that never got to taste it.
[#diy] Next time you tear a slice, look at the holes. Each one was a burp.
