What the fuck does an AI bubble mean, and why should you even care? Because it's not about robots. It's about your money. Picture Maya's lemonade stand. One day, everyone decides lemonade is the future. Strangers start buying pieces of her stand. The price doubles. Then it triples. But she hasn't sold more cups. People are just betting that she will. That's a bubble: the price runs way ahead of the profit. Now swap the lemonade for A.I. Tech giants are pouring around five hundred thirty-nine billion dollars into it this year, mostly before the profits show up. And the company selling the shovels, Nvidia, got so big it's about six to eight percent of the entire S&P five hundred. So if your retirement account holds an index fund, you already own a piece of the stand. Bubbles pop when people stop believing the profits are coming. It's happened before. In two thousand, Cisco was the most valuable company on Earth. Two years later, its stock was down about eighty-five percent. It took twenty five years to climb back. And the internet was real. Now, on November twelfth, Nvidia's C.E.O., Jensen Huang, resigned. The company didn't say why. And the stock kept sliding. Does that pop the bubble? Not by itself. The chips still sell. But bubbles run on confidence, and the face of A.I. just walked off the stage. The tech can be real, and still be overpriced. And your money's already in it.
