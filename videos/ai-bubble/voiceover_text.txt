What the fuck does an AI bubble mean, and why should you care? It's not about robots. It's about your money. Picture Maya's lemonade stand. Everyone decides lemonade is the future. People buy pieces of her stand. The price doubles. Then triples. She hasn't sold more cups. They're just betting she will. That's a bubble: the price runs way ahead of the profit. Now swap lemonade for A.I. Tech giants are pouring about five hundred thirty-nine billion dollars into it this year, before the profits show up. Nvidia, which sells the chips, is now about six to eight percent of the whole S&P five hundred. If your retirement account holds an index fund, you already own a piece of the stand. Bubbles pop when people stop believing the profits are coming. In two thousand, Cisco was the most valuable company on Earth. Two years later, it was down about eighty-five percent. It took twenty five years to climb back. And the internet was real. On November twelfth, Nvidia's C.E.O., Jensen Huang, resigned. The company didn't say why. The stock kept sliding. Does that pop the bubble? Not by itself. But bubbles run on confidence, and the face of A.I. just walked off the stage. The tech can be real, and still be overpriced. And your money's already in it.
