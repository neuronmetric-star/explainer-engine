# Sources: Why motivation quits on you, but discipline doesn't

**Doug, Gary, the shoes, the rain, "seven mornings" and "day twelve" are fictional** (an illustrative story). The habit research is real.
"That 'LET'S GO!' feeling… That's motivation" and "Hates it. Goes anyway. That's discipline." are plain-word definitions, not study claims.

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| "Every morning at six, Gary sees his shoes… After enough mornings like that, just seeing the shoes gets him moving. That's a habit." (like brushing your teeth): habits form when a behavior is repeated in a consistent context, so the context (time, place, cue) comes to trigger the behavior automatically, without deliberate thought | UCL Behavioural Science & Health, "The science of habits": https://www.ucl.ac.uk/epidemiology-health-care/research/behavioural-science-health/research/healthy-habits/science-of-habits · APA Monitor on Wendy Wood's habit research (43% of everyday actions done habitually, in the same context, while thinking of something else): https://apa.org/monitor/2020/11/career-lab-habits |
| "In one London study, it typically took about sixty-six days for a new routine to feel automatic. Some people took longer.": Lally et al. (2010), University College London, *European Journal of Social Psychology*, "How are habits formed." 96 people repeated one daily behavior for 84 days; the **average** time to reach peak automaticity was 66 days (range 18 to a predicted 254). Exercise took about 1.5× longer than eating/drinking habits; one missed day had little effect | UCL news, "How long does it take to form a habit?": https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit · BPS Research Digest: https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit · UCL blog, "Busting the 21 days habit formation myth": https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/bsh/2012/06/29/busting-the-21-days-habit-formation-myth/ |
| "After enough mornings like that, just seeing the shoes gets him moving. That's a habit." (background: the most self-controlled people lean on habits, not effort): Galla & Duckworth (2015), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, "More than resisting temptation": people high in self-control rely *less* on effortful inhibition yet make more goal progress; beneficial habits explain the link between self-control and good outcomes (exercise, eating, sleep, study) | PMC full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4731333 |
| (background, not in narration) Strava's "Quitter's Day": the biggest drop in workouts comes in the second week of January | TechRadar: https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/tired-of-your-busy-january-gym-strava-reveals-the-exact-date-itll-be-quiet-again · Cycling Weekly: https://cyclingweekly.com/news/strava-says-new-years-resolutions-going-waver-now-unless-take-action-365223 |

Notes: "about sixty-six days" is said as an average (individuals varied a lot), which matches the study. We don't say "21 days" (a myth, per UCL).
