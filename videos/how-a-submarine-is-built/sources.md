# Sources — How a US nuclear submarine is built (Virginia-class)

- U.S. Navy Fact File, "Attack Submarines - SSN" — Virginia class: 377 ft, ~7,800 tons submerged, 25+ knots, one nuclear reactor, photonics masts instead of periscopes. https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169558/attack-submarines-ssn/
- Submarine Industrial Base Council, "General Characteristics: Virginia Class" — 377 ft, 7,800 tons, crew 132–135 (15 officers + ~117–120 enlisted). https://submarinesuppliers.org/?p=136
- Congressional Research Service RL32418, "Navy Virginia (SSN-774) Class Attack Submarine Procurement" (Jan 26, 2026) — about $5.0 billion per boat at two per year. https://www.lasg.org/Modernization/CRS-NavyVirginiaClassSubmarineProgram_26Jan2026.pdf
- Seapower / Defense Media Network / General Dynamics Electric Boat — boats built in modules by Electric Boat and HII Newport News as partners; hull cylinders welded at Quonset Point, outfitted, moved between yards by barge; yards alternate final delivery. https://www.gdeb.com/about/oursubmarines/virginia/ , https://defensemedianetwork.com/stories/building-and-improving-virginia-class-ssns
- Naval Technology, "Arkansas SSN reaches pressure hull complete" — sections joined into one pressure hull before final outfitting. https://www.naval-technology.com/news/arkansas-ssn-reaches-pressure-hull-complete/
- Baird Maritime / Marcus Hellyer analysis of Navy data — average predicted construction time 106 months (~8 yr 10 mo); Navy target 60 months. https://www.bairdmaritime.com/security/naval/naval-submersibles/opinion-is-americas-submarine-production-speeding-up-not-according-to-us-navy-data
- Navy-focused weapons summary (via Navy/defense sources) — each boat can carry up to 37 torpedo-sized weapons incl. Tomahawks; 12 in vertical launch. https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2021/07/us-navys-virginia-class-submarines-get-more-tomahawk-missiles/
- GlobalSecurity, "S9G reactor" + Federation of American Scientists, "Life of the Ship Reactors" — reactor designed for ~33 years without refueling. https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/ship/systems/s9g.htm , https://fas.org/wp-content/uploads/media/Life-of-the-Ship-Reactors-and-Accelerated-Testing.pdf
- We Are The Mighty / ScienceABC — nuclear subs desalinate seawater, then electrolysis makes oxygen; food (~90 days typical) and crew are the practical limits. https://www.wearethemighty.com/mighty-tactical/this-is-how-us-subs-make-oxygen-from-seawater/ , https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/how-do-people-get-oxygen-and-drinking-water-inside-a-submarine
- U.S. Navy Fact File (attack submarines) — designed to hunt enemy submarines and surface ships, strike ashore with Tomahawk missiles and special operations forces, and conduct intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance; lockout trunk for divers. https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169558/attack-submarines-ssn/
- Baird Maritime, "Initial sea trials completed for US Navy submarine Massachusetts" + National Interest (Block IV) — can dive to depths greater than 800 feet (exact test depth is classified). https://www.bairdmaritime.com/amp/story/security/naval/naval-submersibles/initial-sea-trials-completed-for-us-navy-submarine-massachusetts
- Physics: seawater adds ~1 atmosphere per ~33 ft, so at 800 ft total pressure ≈ 800/33 + 1 ≈ 25 atmospheres (≈ 370 psi absolute) — "25× the surface".
- DVIDS / U.S. Navy, "USS Virginia returns from deployment" — Virginia-class deployments typically about six months (including port visits). https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/488132
- ~90 days of food stores: ScienceABC (crews typically load ~90 days of rations) and ssn706.northwest.net ("endurance 90 days, limited by stores, not fuel") — secondary sources; script says "about".
