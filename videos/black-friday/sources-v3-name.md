# Sources: Why is it called Black Friday?

**Doug, Gary and "Doug as a 1961 Philadelphia traffic cop" are a reconstruction.** The history is real.

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| Black Friday is the day after Thanksgiving (the US holiday on the fourth Thursday of November); stores cut prices, shoppers line up early, and it's the unofficial start of the Christmas shopping season | Britannica, "Black Friday (retail)": https://www.britannica.com/topic/Black-Friday-retail · History.com (below) |
| Red ink for losses, black ink for profits; "in the black" = making a profit | Britannica, "Why Is It Called Black Friday?": https://www.britannica.com/story/why-is-it-called-black-friday |
| The oldest known written version of the "stores go from red to black" explanation is from 1981 (Philadelphia Inquirer) | Snopes, "Black Friday": https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/black-friday-2 · History.com, "What's the Real History of Black Friday?": https://www.history.com/articles/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history |
| The name is about twenty years older: Philadelphia police used it from the late 1950s / early 1960s | Philadelphia Inquirer, "Did Philly cops coin 'Black Friday'?": https://www.inquirer.com/philly/news/breaking/20111125_Black_Friday_started_in_Philly.html · Britannica (above) · CBS Philadelphia: https://www.cbsnews.com/philadelphia/news/the-little-known-philadelphia-origins-of-black-friday |
| The day after Thanksgiving: suburban shoppers and tourists flooded the city; the Army–Navy football game was played in Philadelphia that Saturday | History.com (above) · Inquirer (above) |
| No traffic officer could take the day off; 12-hour shifts | Inquirer (above) · CBS Philadelphia (above) |
| Merchants tried to rename it "Big Friday" (around 1961); it didn't stick | Britannica (above) · NBC, "Why is it called Black Friday? Here are a few theories": https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/national-international/why-is-it-called-black-friday-here-are-a-few-theories/3058284/ |
| The profit version spread nationally in the late 1980s, promoted by retailers | Britannica (above) · Fortune, "Black Friday history": https://fortune.com/2017/11/21/black-friday-2017-thanksgiving-holiday-shopping-history/ |
| *(For comments, not in the script)* The first recorded "Black Friday" was the Sept 24, 1869 gold-market crash (Jay Gould and Jim Fisk) | Britannica, "Black Friday (1869)": https://www.britannica.com/event/Black-Friday · History.com (above) |
| *(For comments, not in the script)* The claim that the name comes from slave sales after Thanksgiving is false | History.com (above) · CNN Money, "Black Friday history": https://money.cnn.com/2014/11/28/news/black-friday-history/ · Fortune (above) |
