Why the fuck do we dream, and why do we forget them? Dreams aren't random noise. Your brain is working the night shift. Here's what scientists think. Jay sleeps eight hours. About two of them are spent dreaming, mostly in REM sleep. Step one: replay. His brain replays the day, sorting what to keep. Step two: rehearse. One theory says dreams simulate threats, so you practice escaping danger safely. Step three: delete. In a 2019 mouse study, special neurons helped the brain forget during REM. And here's why dreams vanish. The hippocampus, your memory saver, is one of the last parts to wake up. And noradrenaline, a chemical that helps lock memories in, runs low during REM. So Jay wakes up, and the dream sits in short-term memory with nothing to save it. Seconds later, it's gone. It's like writing an essay with autosave turned off, and then the laptop dies. You don't forget your dreams. Your brain just never hit save. Follow for more why the fuck science.
