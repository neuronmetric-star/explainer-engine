Why the fuck did China build a wall over thirteen thousand miles long? It's not one wall. It's thousands of walls, built over about two thousand years. Meet Li, a soldier on China's northern border. Out there: nomad horsemen. Fast raiders. Step one: slow them down. Horses can't jump a wall. Raiders had to stop or find a gate. Step two: sound the alarm. Beacon towers passed smoke signals, tower to tower. Step three: control the border. Every gate let China tax traders and decide who got in. Around two fourteen B C, the first emperor linked older walls. Most of today's wall is Ming. There's just one weakness: the people guarding it. In sixteen forty-four, a Ming general opened a gate and let the Manchus in. Add every wall from every dynasty: twenty-one thousand kilometers. It's like the lock on your front door. It won't stop a pro. It buys you time. The wall wasn't built to stop everyone. Just to stop them long enough. Follow for more why the fuck history.
