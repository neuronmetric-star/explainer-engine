# Sources: Why the heck do you spend more when you pay with a card?

**Doug, Gary, their bids of "thirty" and "sixty", the wince and the fries simile are our reconstruction.** The auction, its
cash-vs-card split and the average bids are a real experiment (row 1); the brain-scan finding is real (row 4).

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| "This auction really happened, at MIT": a sealed-bid auction for Boston Celtics tickets; half the bidders had to pay cash, half by credit card | Prelec & Simester (2001), "Always Leave Home Without It: A Further Investigation of the Credit-Card Effect on Willingness to Pay," *Marketing Letters* 12(1). MIT course slides: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-821-listening-to-the-customer-fall-2002/5cc52570803b6e1bbea64585acdce779_redblack20copy.pdf · Inverse summary: https://inverse.com/culture/59670-credit-card-psychology |
| "Cash people bid about twenty-nine dollars. Card people? About sixty-one. Double." | Same: average bids $29 (cash) vs $61 (card), significant at p<.01 (MIT slides); Inverse reports $28.51 vs $60.64, a 113% "credit card premium". **Caveat:** the paper says the effect isn't always present (a second test, a dinner prize, didn't show it) |
| "Handing over cash hurts a little… Scientists call that the pain of paying" | Prelec & Loewenstein (1998), "The Red and the Black," *Marketing Science* 17(1): purchases cause an immediate "pain of paying"; credit cards weaken the link between buying and paying, cash keeps it tight. https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v17y1998i1p4-28.html · Overview: https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_of_paying (term from Zellermayer 1996) |
| "The bill shows up next month, when the fun's over": card payment is delayed and separated from the purchase ("decoupling") | Prelec & Loewenstein (1998), as above |
| "In a 2021 brain scan, just seeing their card lit up people's reward center… It has a gas pedal": fMRI study; credit-card purchases strongly activated the striatum (reward network) at the moment the card cue appeared, not tied to price; cash purchases only weakly. Authors: cards "step on the gas" rather than only "release the brakes" | Banker, Dunfield, Huang & Prelec (2021), "Neural mechanisms of credit card spending," *Scientific Reports* 11:4070. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33603078/ · full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7892835 · MIT Sloan: https://mitsloan.mit.edu/experts/how-credit-cards-activate-reward-center-our-brains-and-drive-spending . **Caveat:** small sample; doesn't rule out the pain-of-paying effect too |
| "the "ooh, treat" part. Like smelling fries": the striatum is part of the brain's reward system (our plain-words simile) | same 2021 paper (reward network) |
