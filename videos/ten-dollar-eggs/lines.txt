How many eggs could ten dollars buy over the last 80 years?
Meet Ray. Same ten-dollar bill, same grocery store, every decade since 1950.
1950. Eggs cost about 60 cents a dozen. Ray carries out 16 dozen.
1960: 17 dozen. 1970: still 16.
1980. Prices jump. Down to 11.
1990: 9 dozen.
2000. Eggs actually got cheaper. Back up to 10.
2010: 6. 2020: still 6.
Today, a dozen costs 2 dollars 32. Ray gets 4.
From 16 dozen to 4. Three quarters of the cart, gone.
But there's a catch. Ray's paycheck changed too.
In 1950, minimum wage was 75 cents. That ten dollars took over 13 hours of work.
Today, it takes about an hour and 23 minutes.
So ten dollars buys fewer eggs than ever. But one hour of work buys more.
Follow for more money, so simple a 5-year-old gets it.
