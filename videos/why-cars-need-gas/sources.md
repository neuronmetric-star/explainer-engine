# Sources: Why a car needs gasoline

The story (Doug, Gary, the hose, the tow) is **fictional**. The mechanism and the numbers are real:

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| "two-ton car": the average new US vehicle weighs about 4,354 lb (model year 2024) | EPA Automotive Trends Report 2025, as reported by Jalopnik: https://jalopnik.com/2215966/heres-how-much-average-car-weighs · EPA highlights: https://www.epa.gov/automotive-trends/highlights-automotive-trends-report |
| Four-stroke cycle: intake of the fuel-air mix, compression by the piston, spark plug ignition pushes the piston down, crankshaft turns the motion into rotation, exhaust out | Haynes, "What is a four-stroke engine": https://haynes.com/en-us/tips-tutorials/what-four-stroke-engine · TestingAutos four-stroke explainer: https://www.testingautos.com/car_care/four-stroke-engine.html |
| "about fifteen times its weight in air": the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio for gasoline is 14.7:1 by mass (about 14.1:1 for E10) | Wikipedia, Air–fuel ratio: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air%E2%80%93fuel_ratio · AA1Car: https://www.aa1car.com/library/air_fuel_ratios.htm |
| "about 80 times a second": each cylinder of a four-stroke fires once every 2 crank revolutions. At a 2,500 rpm cruise, 4 cylinders × 2,500 ÷ 2 = 5,000 firings per minute ≈ 83 per second (**our calculation**; real rpm varies by car and speed) | Firing interval per four-stroke sources above |
| Water is denser than gasoline, sinks to the bottom of the tank where the fuel pump pickup sits, and causes misfires or stalling; a tank with a lot of water is usually drained by a shop | Jalopnik, "What could happen if water gets in your gas tank": https://jalopnik.com/2012488/water-in-gas-tank-what-happens/ · Kwik Fit: https://kwik-fit.com/blog/what-happens-when-water-gets-into-fuel-tank |
| "less than a third of each boom moves you, the rest is mostly heat": only about 12%–30% of a gasoline car's fuel energy moves it down the road; most of the loss is engine heat | US DOE / EPA fueleconomy.gov, "Where the energy goes": https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/atv.shtml |
| "The car only started on the gas still left in the lines" | **Story device / simplification.** Gas already in the fuel lines and rail can briefly run an engine before contaminated fuel arrives; how long varies by car. It's presented as part of the fictional story. |
