# Sources — Why do we shower in the morning if we showered at night?

- NBC News / NIH skin microbiome survey (2009), "Your skin is a giant germ factory" — roughly 100 billion bacteria live on skin; ~1,000 species. https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/eeeww-your-skin-giant-germ-factory-flna1c9444445 · NHGRI release: https://www.genome.gov/news/news-release/nih-researchers-find-thousands-of-new-microorganisms-living-on-human-skin
- Tom's Guide, "Why do I sweat so much in my sleep? A sleep scientist answers" — body releases heat through the skin to fall/stay asleep; overnight sweating. https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/mattresses/why-do-i-sweat-in-my-sleep
- Connect Savannah, "Way more than you wanted to know about bed sweat" — ~200 ml of moisture per 8 hours of sleep (much of it exhaled) ≈ a cup; larger "1 liter" claims are exaggerated. https://www.connectsavannah.com/extras/way-more-than-you-wanted-to-know-about-bed-sweat-2134349
- Biology Insights / multiple — adults shed ~30,000–40,000 dead skin cells per minute (estimate; script says "tens of thousands"); millions over a night. https://biologyinsights.com/how-many-pounds-of-skin-do-we-shed/
- Dust mites live in mattresses and pillows and feed on shed skin cells (same sources; also standard allergy guidance).
- University of York / Scientific Reports (2020), via Yorkshire Post — Staphylococcus hominis converts an odorless sweat molecule (S-Cys-Gly-3M3SH) into 3M3SH, a sulfurous thioalcohol: the main armpit smell. https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/health/scientists-have-discovered-exactly-what-causes-body-odour-2926092 · Science News Explores explainer: https://www.snexplores.org/article/explainer-bacteria-behind-your-bo
- Mental Floss, "What causes morning breath?" + NutritionFacts summary — saliva drops during sleep; oral bacteria produce volatile sulfur compounds. https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/51348/what-causes-morning-breath
- Pocket/Popular Science, "Save your skin: how you shower matters more than when, dermatologists say"; Newsweek dermatologist roundup — timing is personal; short, lukewarm showers and gentle cleanser; hot water / overwashing can dry skin. https://getpocket.com/explore/item/save-your-skin-how-you-shower-matters-more-than-when-dermatologists-say · https://www.newsweek.com/better-shower-morning-night-heres-what-experts-say-11702858
