# Sources: How does a fridge work?

**Doug, Gary, the chair and the Thanksgiving kitchen are fictional.** The physics is real.

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| "How does a fridge work" is TikTok's top search suggestion for "how does a fridge" | Scrape Creators, TikTok search suggestions, Nov 22 2026 (score 0.088) |
| A fridge doesn't make cold; it moves heat from inside to the kitchen | Penn State EGEE 102, "Refrigerators": https://courses.ems.psu.edu/egee102/node/2402 · HowStuffWorks: https://home.howstuffworks.com/refrigerator.htm/printable |
| The pipes carry a liquid called refrigerant; in the pipes inside the fridge (the evaporator) it boils and soaks up heat from the food and air | Danfoss, "How a refrigerator works": https://www.danfoss.com/en-us/service-and-support/fix-and-troubleshooting/air-condition-and-refrigeration-installer-hub/the-fridge-how-it-works/ · iFixit, "How Refrigerators Work": https://www.ifixit.com/News/31122/how-refrigerators-work |
| "It boils at about eleven degrees": isobutane (R-600a), the refrigerant in most new fridges, boils at −11.7 °C (10.9 °F) at normal air pressure | refrigeranthq, R-600a fact sheet: https://refrigeranthq.com/r-600a-isobutane-refrigerant-fact-info-sheet/ · Danfoss R600a guide: https://assets.danfoss.com/documents/latest/27733/AN000086421702en-000101.pdf (US fridges may use other refrigerants; we say "about") |
| "Water needs two hundred twelve": water boils at 212 °F at sea level | standard reference value |
| Boiling/evaporating soaks up heat, like sweat cooling your skin | Penn State EGEE 102 (above) |
| A pump (the compressor) squeezes the gas, which makes it hot | iFixit (above) · Danfoss (above) |
| Pipes in the sides/back (the condenser) dump that heat into the kitchen; that's why the side of a fridge feels warm | iFixit wiki: https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/How_a_Refrigerator_Works · RTINGS: https://www.rtings.com/refrigerator/learn/how-refrigerators-work |
| An open fridge door makes the kitchen warmer over time: the heat goes back into the room, plus the compressor's own heat | University of Illinois Physics Van, "Open Refrigerator": https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/1790 · Indiana Public Media, A Moment of Science: https://indianapublicmedia.org/amomentofscience/heating-kitchen-refrigerator.php |
