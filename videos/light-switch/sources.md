the "instant" (push speed) row, the filament row, the LED row, and the burnout row below. Other rows are for the long draft.

The power plant "thirty miles away" is an illustrative distance. The physics is real.

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| A switch opens/closes a gap (air) in the circuit; current only flows around a complete loop; air is a poor conductor | SparkFun, "Button and Switch Basics": https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/button-and-switch-basics/what-is-a-switch · Dummies, "Closed, Open, and Short Circuits": https://www.dummies.com/article/technology/electronics/general-electronics/closed-open-and-short-circuits-141399 · schoolphysics, "Switches": https://schoolphysics.co.uk/age11-14/Electricity%20and%20magnetism/Current%20electricity/text/Switches_/index.html |
| Copper has loose (free) electrons; "billions of trillions" per inch of house wire | OpenStax University Physics Vol. 2, §9.2 "Model of Conduction in Metals" (copper ≈ 8.5×10²⁸ free electrons per m³): https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-2-model-of-conduction-in-metals. Our calculation: 14-gauge wire (2.08 mm²) × 1 inch ≈ 4.5×10²¹ = 4.5 billion trillion. |
| The push (signal) travels at 50–99% of the speed of light; electrons themselves drift slowly | Wikipedia, "Speed of electricity": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_electricity · Wikipedia, "Velocity factor": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity_factor. Yardstick (ours): half of light speed = 150,000 km/s; Earth's circumference ≈ 40,000 km → 3.75 laps per second. Living room ~5 m ÷ 1.5×10⁸ m/s ≈ 33 billionths of a second. |
| "A lamp pushes him about two hundredths of a millimeter per second… about fifty times slower than a snail… about three days to cross the room" | Drift-velocity formula and worked example (12-gauge, 20 A ≈ 0.44 mm/s): OpenStax (above) and College Physics Answers: https://collegephysicsanswers.com/openstax-solutions/using-results-example-203-find-drift-velocity-copper-wire-twice-diameter-and. **Our calculation** for a 60 W bulb (0.5 A) in 14-gauge wire: v = I/(nAe) ≈ 0.018 mm/s. Garden snail ≈ 1 mm/s (Biology Insights: https://biologyinsights.com/how-fast-does-a-snail-move-snail-speed-explained/) → ≈ 55× slower. 15 ft (4.57 m) ÷ 0.018 mm/s ≈ 3 days. |
| US household power is alternating current at 60 Hz: it reverses direction, 60 cycles every second (~120 V) | HyperPhysics, "Household Use of Electric Energy": https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/electric/hsehld2.html · OpenStax College Physics §20.5 "Alternating Current versus Direct Current" (via theexpertta.com PDF): https://theexpertta.com/book-files/OpenStaxCollegePhysics/CP_20.5.%20Alternating%20Current%20versus%20Direct%20Current_pg861-866.pdf |
| Power plants don't send electrons; a generator moves a magnet relative to coils of wire, which pushes electrons in the wire; most plants spin it with steam turbines, others with falling water or wind | U.S. EIA, "Magnets and electricity": https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/magnets-and-electricity.php · U.S. EIA, "How electricity is generated": https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/how-electricity-is-generated.php |
| Incandescent filament is tungsten (highest melting point of any metal), heated to ~2,500–3,000 K (≈ 4,000–4,900 °F) so it glows white; roughly 90% of the energy becomes heat | ScienceABC, "How do light bulbs work": https://www.scienceabc.com/innovation/how-do-bulbs-work · Popular Science, "How bad are incandescent light bulbs for the environment?": https://www.popsci.com/environment/light-bulbs-sustainability-energy/ |
| "LEDs need at least seventy-five percent less push, and last up to twenty-five times longer" | U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Saver, "LED Lighting": "Residential LEDs -- especially ENERGY STAR rated products -- use at least 75% less energy, and last up to 25 times longer, than incandescent lighting." https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/led-lighting |
| Ovens top out around 500 °F | General knowledge (typical home oven max 500–550 °F); used only as a yardstick. |

Notes: the marble-in-a-tube and garden-hose comparisons are analogies (simplifications) for the push traveling
through conductors already full of electrons. Strictly, the energy travels in the electromagnetic field guided by
the wires; "the push" is our plain-word name for it.
