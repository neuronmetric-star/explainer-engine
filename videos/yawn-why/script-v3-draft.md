## Narration
You've yawned today, and scientists still can't agree why we yawn. It's not for air. And it's more than being sleepy.

See, Doug had a problem. He couldn't stop yawning, and he worried something was wrong. One yawn was so wide, a pigeon checked for vacancy. Gary slept right through it.

Honestly? I believed the air thing too. The idea: a yawn gulps in extra air. So Doug breathed pure oxygen.

Perfect! Except... scientists tested that in 1987. Pure oxygen changed nothing.

So Doug ran laps, to breathe twice as fast. Perfect! Except... same result.

So if it's not air... what is it?

Here's the best idea so far. Think of your brain like a laptop. It gets warm when it works. A yawn may pull in cool air, like a little fan. Not every scientist agrees.

So, does it fit? Researchers watched 160 people. When the air was hotter than their bodies, they yawned less. Makes sense: hot air can't cool you off.

In rats, tiny probes showed the brain heating up before a yawn, and cooling down after. Heat first, then a yawn, then cooler.

So it's heat, not air or sleep. Almost. When Gary finally yawns, Doug yawns too. Why we catch each other's yawns is still a mystery.

So, why do we yawn? Best guess: to cool a warm brain. Nobody's sure.

Nothing's wrong with Doug. Probably. Next yawn, feel the cool air.

Send this to someone who's yawning.
