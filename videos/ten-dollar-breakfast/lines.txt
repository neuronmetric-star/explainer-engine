How much breakfast could ten dollars buy over the last 80 years?
Meet Ray. Every decade, he takes the same ten-dollar bill to the store.
His list never changes: a loaf of bread, a dozen eggs, a gallon of milk.
1950. That list costs about a dollar 57. Ray walks out with 6 full bags.
1960: 5 bags. 1970: 4.
1980. Prices jump. Ray can't even afford 3.
1990 and 2000: 2 bags each.
2010. Down to 1. 2020: still 1.
Today, the list costs 8 dollars 28. Ray gets 1 bag and a dollar 72 in change.
Six bags, down to one.
But there's a catch. Ray's paycheck changed too.
In 1950, minimum wage was 75 cents. One bag took over 2 hours of work.
Today, it takes just over 1 hour.
So ten dollars buys less food than ever. But an hour of work buys more.
Follow for more money, so simple a 5-year-old gets it.
