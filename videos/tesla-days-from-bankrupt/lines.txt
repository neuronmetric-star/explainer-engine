December 24th, 2008. 6 p.m. Christmas Eve.
Tesla has almost no money left.
In 2 days, its workers' paychecks would bounce.
Its boss has put in nearly everything he owns.
He says he even borrowed from friends to pay rent.
Then, in the last hour, the deal closes.
12 years later, Tesla is the most valuable car company on Earth.
Worth more than Toyota, the company that sells the most cars in the world.
So how does a company 3 days from dying end up on top?
And why didn't the man who saved it even start it?
It starts in a tiny office in California.
July 2003. 2 engineers, Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning, start a car company.
They name it after the inventor Nikola Tesla.
Their idea: an electric car that's fast and fun, not slow and boxy.
But building cars costs a fortune. They need an investor.
Early 2004, they pitch a young internet millionaire.
“We want to make electric cars cool.” (dramatised)
“Fast and cool? Then I'm in.” (dramatised)
He puts in $6.5 million of the first $7.5 million raised.
He becomes chairman, the head of the board.
But he's not the CEO. Not yet.
Tesla's first car, the Roadster, starts at $109,000.
That's on purpose. Elon writes a “secret master plan.”
Step 1: build a pricey sports car.
Step 2: use that money to build a cheaper car. Then an even cheaper one.
Like selling fancy lemonade first, to pay for a bigger stand.
But building it is much harder than planned.
In 2007, the board pushes founder Martin Eberhard out as CEO.
In 2008, Roadsters are finally built, in an old car dealer's repair bays.
By January 2009, Tesla has delivered just 147 cars.
And just then, the whole economy crashes.
Fall 2008. Big banks fail. Even America's biggest carmakers are going broke.
Who lends money to a tiny electric-car startup now? Nobody.
Tesla's big fundraising round falls apart.
October 2008: Elon makes himself CEO.
And his other company is in trouble too.
SpaceX rockets have failed 3 times in a row.
In his words, there's money for 1 last launch.
September 28, 2008: the 4th rocket reaches orbit.
But Tesla is still bleeding cash.
So Elon puts in the last of his PayPal money.
He says he didn't even own a house anymore.
Now he needs his investors to chip in too.
The deal is an IOU that can later turn into a slice of the company.
The total: about $40 million.
Reports say one big investor tries to slow it down.
The clock ticks toward Christmas.
6 p.m., December 24th: the deal closes.
In Elon's words, payroll would have bounced 2 days later.
That same December, NASA hands SpaceX a $1.6 billion contract.
Both companies survive. Barely.
2010: the U.S. government lends Tesla $465 million.
June 29, 2010: Tesla starts selling shares to the public at $17 each.
That makes the whole company worth about $1.5 billion.
Think of a pizza: 1 slice's price times the number of slices.
Tesla buys an old car factory in Fremont, California, for $42 million.
It used to belong to GM and Toyota together.
June 2012: the first Model S family cars go to customers.
2013: Tesla pays back the government loan, 9 years early.
Now for step 3 of the plan.
March 31, 2016: Elon shows the cheaper Model 3.
In 1 week, about 325,000 people put down roughly $1,000 each.
Now Tesla has to actually build them. Fast.
Elon promises 5,000 cars a week.
In spring 2018, Tesla averages about 2,200 a week, less than half the goal.
The robots keep getting stuck.
Elon later admits Tesla relied too much on robots.
He spends nights sleeping inside the factory.
So the team builds a new assembly line inside a giant tent.
Elon says it went up in about 2 weeks.
Last week of June 2018: they make 5,031 Model 3s.
But the danger was real.
Elon says Tesla came “about a month” from bankruptcy.
He calls those 2 years “production and logistics hell.”
Then the cars start flowing, and so does the money. 4 profitable quarters in a row, a first.
July 1, 2020: Tesla passes Toyota, worth about $209 billion.
12 years after Christmas Eve, it's the most valuable carmaker on Earth.
Today, Tesla is worth about $1.5 trillion.
That's roughly 1,000 times its value on day 1.
Toyota is worth about $253 billion, so Tesla is worth about 6 times more.
Yet in 2025, Toyota sold about 11.3 million cars.
Tesla sold about 1.6 million.
Why? Because a stock price is a bet on tomorrow, not today.
People are paying for the lemonade stand they think it will become.
Will that bet pay off? Nobody knows yet.
Back in 2008, Elon put in his last money…
…“even though I thought we would still fail.”
Follow for more money, so simple a 5-year-old gets it.
