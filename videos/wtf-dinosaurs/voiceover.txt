Why the fuck did one rock kill the dinosaurs? It wasn't the hit. It was the darkness. Here's how it works. Sixty-six million years ago, Tina the Triceratops is eating ferns. Then a rock about ten kilometers wide slams into Mexico at up to seventy-two thousand kilometers an hour. It leaves a crater about one hundred eighty kilometers wide. Step one: fire. Debris rains back down, the sky heats up, and forests burn. Step two: darkness. Soot, dust and sulfur block the sun. Plants get under one percent of normal light for over a year. Step three: starvation. No sun, no plants. No plants, no Tina. No Tina, no T rex. About seventy-five percent of all species, gone. But there's one twist. Not every dinosaur died. Birds made it. Scientists think beaks let them eat seeds. Meanwhile, tiny mammals hid in burrows. Those were our ancestors. The asteroid didn't kill the dinosaurs. It turned off the sun.
