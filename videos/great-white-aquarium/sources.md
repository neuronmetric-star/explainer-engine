# Sources: why are there no great white sharks in aquariums

Mode: **True story** (Brian, Nov 25 2026). Search phrase: **why are there no great white sharks in aquariums**.
Payoff (one sentence): a great white is built to cross oceans and feel tiny electric buzzes, so a tank makes it stop eating,
ram the walls, and (if it does survive) eat its tank mates; every one ever kept died or was let go.
Note: direct page fetches were blocked by the session network; facts below were confirmed across several search results.
Where sources disagree, the script uses the safe version (in **bold**).

Script says: "tiny holes on its nose feel the electric buzz of a heartbeat". | [UT Marine Science Institute](https://utmsi.utexas.edu/science-and-the-sea/radio-program/ampullae-of-lorenzini/) · [FAU Shark Lab](https://www.fau.edu/science/shark-lab/electroreception/) |
| 4 | SeaWorld, 1981: a great white kept **16 days**, then released (it wasn't eating). | 5 | Big open-ocean sharks breathe by swimming: water has to rush over the gills (ram ventilation), so they can never stop moving. | [UF/IFAS FA179](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA179) · [Born Free USA](https://www.bornfreeusa.org/?p=25833) |
