# Sources: Why does the guy who keeps missing hit the target first? (Brian's script)

**Fable.** Doug, Gary, the bow range and the three sliding barriers are fictional; the script makes no factual claims.
The lesson it illustrates (early attempts that fail, then adjusting from what each miss shows, beat waiting for the
perfect moment) is consistent with research on learning from errors:

| Idea in the story | Background |
|---|---|
| Gary's misses each tell him something (too early → keep the timing, change the aim) | "Productive failure" (Manu Kapur): learners who attempt a problem first, fail and then get instruction outperform those taught first, especially on transfer. Meta-analysis of 53 studies / 166 comparisons. Imperial College blog interview: https://blogs.imperial.ac.uk/education-research/?p=374 · Kapur, Saba & Roll (2023), *npj Science of Learning*: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-023-00165-y.pdf · overview: https://teachingtimes.com/learning-from-productive-failure |
| Caveat | Failure helps only when you learn from it and build on it; it doesn't work in every setting ("When productive failure fails", Kapur et al.). Same sources. |
