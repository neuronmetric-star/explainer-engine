Why the heck did Bill Gates and Mark Zuckerberg leave Harvard before graduating?
Bill Gates left Harvard before graduating and went on to build Microsoft, the company behind Windows. Mark Zuckerberg also left Harvard before graduating to grow Facebook, the social network he started in his dorm.
Two dropouts. Two famous companies. Honestly? I figured they just got lucky.
In 1975, nineteen-year-old Gates sees a magazine cover: the Altair, a build-it-yourself computer. No screen. Just switches and blinking lights. A toaster with ambition.
Gates and his friend Paul Allen write software for it. They've never even touched an Altair. They write the code on a big Harvard computer that pretends to be one.
It works. The Altair's maker agrees to pay for the software. A real customer.
Then Gates leaves Harvard and builds Microsoft with Allen—the company behind Windows.
Twenty-nine years later, Zuckerberg, also nineteen, builds Facebook in his Harvard dorm room. In a month, more than half the college signs up. By summer, Facebook has over a hundred thousand student users. A football stadium.
More than a year later, Zuckerberg leaves Harvard to focus on Facebook.
Customer first. Stadium first. Dropping out came last.
They didn't drop out to find something. They'd already found it. So much for lucky.
One study found that founders of the fastest-growing U.S. companies averaged forty-five years old. Twice as old as these two. Because by then, you know what people will pay for.
And a separate study found that about nine in ten U.S.-born tech founders had bachelor's degrees.
Harvard gave them both honorary degrees later. Gates got his thirty-two years later. Zuckerberg got his twelve.
Build it first. School can wait. Thirty-two years, apparently.
