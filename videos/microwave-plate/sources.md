# Sources: How does a microwave work?

**Doug, Gary, the rattling plate and the burrito are fictional.** The physics and numbers are real.

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| "How does a microwave work" is TikTok's top search suggestion for "how does a microwave" | Scrape Creators, TikTok search suggestions, Nov 22 2026 (score 0.10) |
| No fire, nothing glows; the air inside stays at room temperature (why food doesn't brown) | USDA FSIS, "Cooking Safely in the Microwave Oven": https://www.fsis.usda.gov/es/node/3532 |
| The oven fires invisible waves (microwaves, a kind of radio wave) at 2.45 GHz | IEEE Technology Navigator, "Microwave ovens": https://technav.ieee.org/topic/microwave-ovens/ · McGill Office for Science and Society: https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/general/device-causes-water-molecules-flip-back-and-forth-some-245-billion-times-second-what-it |
| The waves flip water molecules back and forth 2.45 billion times a second; the molecules jostle each other and that makes heat ("rubbing" is our everyday simplification of molecular friction/collisions) | McGill OSS (above) · IEEE (above) · USC Illumin, "The engineering behind the microwave oven": https://illumin.usc.edu/the-engineering-behind-the-microwave-oven/ |
| The waves bounce off the metal walls and make a standing-wave pattern: hot spots where they stack, cold spots where they cancel | Make Tech Easier, "How microwave ovens heat food": https://maketecheasier.com/how-microwave-ovens-heat-food/ · USPTO patent 12648056 (nodes and antinodes in the cavity) |
| Hot spots about 2.4 in (6.1 cm) apart: half of the 12.2 cm wavelength at 2.45 GHz ("a thumb's length" is our yardstick; real ovens vary) | McGill OSS (12.2 cm wavelength) · University of Waterloo Chem 13 News, letter on the chocolate test (theoretical 6.1 cm; real spacings vary): https://uwaterloo.ca/chem13-news-magazine/node/619 |
| The spinning plate moves food through the hot spots so it heats more evenly; a still plate leaves cold spots | Make Tech Easier (above) · Wikipedia, Microwave oven (turntable history): https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radarange |
| Lava outside, frozen inside: the waves only reach 1–1.5 in into food; the middle heats more slowly by conduction | USDA FSIS (above) |
| "Take the plate out, chocolate bar on a dish, twenty seconds. Melted spots": the classic home experiment (turntable removed, chocolate or cheese, ~20 s) | Open University, "Measure the speed of light with your microwave": https://www.open.edu/openlearn/science-maths-technology/science/physics-and-astronomy/physics/measure-the-speed-light-your-microwave · University of Arizona Optical Sciences outreach: https://wp.optics.arizona.edu/oscoutreach/measure-the-speed-of-light-with-a-microwave · Waterloo Chem 13 News: https://uwaterloo.ca/chem13-news-magazine/node/579 |
