# SHORT (~1:10). How an electric car runs. Brian's script (approved), marked up with cues.
# Payoff: no engine: battery (under the floor) → little computer → motor (magnets spin a shaft) → simple gearbox → wheels;
# slowing down, the motor works as a generator and refills the battery a little. Doug is fictional.
[#q] How the {*heck|heck} does an electric car even {*run?|run?}
[#card] Doug has a problem. He bought an electric car, [#hood] popped the hood, and couldn't find the {!engine.|engine.}
[#steal] "Did they steal it?"
[#bet] Honestly, Doug, I'd check the security cameras too.
[#gas] A gas car burns fuel to make power. [#battery] An all-electric car stores energy in a big {*battery|battery} pack, usually underneath the floor. [#plug] Plug it in, and the battery stores electricity for the trip.
[#pedal] Now Doug presses the pedal. [#computer] That pedal tells a little computer, "More power, please."
[#flow] The computer sends electricity from the battery to the {*motor.|motor.} [#magnets] The motor uses magnetic forces to spin a shaft. [#gearbox] That spinning motion goes through a simple gearbox and turns the {*wheels.|wheels.}
[#gentle] Press gently, and the motor turns slowly. [#harder] Press harder, and it gets more power and spins faster.
[#trip] That's the whole trip: battery to motor, motor to wheels.
[#regen] And when Doug slows down, the motor can work like a generator. [#back] It grabs some of the car's motion and sends a little energy back into the battery. [#refill] It's like getting a tiny refill every time he brakes.
[#reveal] So, where's the engine? Doug was looking for the wrong thing. [#motor2] The electric motor is the part that makes the car move, and it has far fewer moving parts than a gas engine.
[#end] Doug found it. He still makes the "vroom" noise himself.
