# Sources: printer-how-it-works ("How does a printer work?") · one-off explainer

Researched Nov 24 2026 (web search). Search phrase: "how does a printer work" (home inkjet).

## Fiction / reconstruction (labeled)
- Doug, Gary and the dog photo: **invented characters** (Doug acts out "you" on screen).
- Comparisons (kettle spitting, ketchup cap crusting shut, sprinkles that look like one color) are teaching analogies.
- We explain THERMAL inkjet (the heater-and-bubble kind: HP, Canon). Some brands (e.g. Epson) squeeze the ink with a flexing crystal instead;
  the script says "most home printers… a tiny heater" only in the sense of "inside millions of printers", never "all printers".

## Facts in the script
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| The computer (printer driver) turns the image into a dot pattern; rollers feed one sheet; the print head rides on a belt-driven carriage across the page; after each pass the paper advances a little and the head sweeps again | HowStuffWorks, "How Inkjet Printers Work": https://computer.howstuffworks.com/inkjet-printer.htm · Wikipedia "Inkjet printing": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkjet_printing |
| A tiny resistor (heater) behind each nozzle superheats the ink (~280–300 °C for water-based ink); a vapor bubble forms and shoves a drop out of the nozzle; the bubble collapses and fresh ink refills. The whole bubble cycle takes ~10 microseconds; a nozzle can refire after ~100–500 microseconds (thousands of times a second) | US patent 6,315,398 "Thermal ink jet heater design": https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6315398 · Wikipedia, "Inkjet printing" (thermal DOD): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkjet_printing |
| Drops are ~2–5 picoliters, about 10–30 microns across; nozzles ~10 microns: thinner than a human hair (≈ 50–100 microns) | US patent 9,738,095 (drop volumes 2–5 pL): https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9738095 · Wikipedia "Inkjet printing" |
| Printers don't mix inks: they lay tiny dots of cyan, magenta, yellow and black side by side; when dots are small, the eye blends them into smooth colors | RIT Digital Print Preservation, "Dot patterns": https://www.rit.edu/ipi/dp3/illustrated-glossary-dot-patterns · RIT, "Printing colors": https://www.rit.edu/ipi/dp3/illustrated-glossary-printing-colors · Wikipedia "CMYK color model": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMYK_color_model |
| 1977: Canon engineer Ichiro Endo's team saw ink shoot out of a syringe when it was accidentally heated with a soldering iron, the start of Canon's "Bubble Jet"; HP (John Vaught's team) found the same idea independently around the same time | Canon, "Printing technology history": https://global.canon/en/intellectual-property/history/printing.html · Optica, Ichiro Endo biography: https://www.optica.org/History/Biographies/bios/Ichiro_Endo · Wikipedia "Inkjet printing" |
