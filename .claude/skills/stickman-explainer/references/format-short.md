# Shorts format (45-70 s, 16:9 by channel default)

This structure is adapted from studying Rick's Clips' animated explainers: the 20 most recent have a 9.7M median, and outliers run 3-3.6× that. It keeps their mechanics and drops their wording, characters and look.

## What the research showed (Rick's Clips, 26 Shorts, 10 transcripts)

- **The opening line is an odd action already happening**, done by an unnamed "he". Examples: "He poured cooking oil into the sea…", "He put a bronze ball in a dragon's mouth…".
- **A doubter or crowd mocks the choice**, which gives the viewer a side to take.
- **Persistence and an escalating threat lead to a visible payoff** (the palace collapses, the rail buckles, the wave hits the pirates). That payoff appeared in 4 of the top 4.
- **The mechanism is explained in the last 10-15 s**, in 2-3 plain sentences. The science term is named once at most.
- **The ending is one image line, with no moral:** "on paper, nobody gambled."
- **Best length: 58-67 s, with one mechanism.**
- **The weakest videos (0.01-0.03× median)** opened with a bare question and no character, ran 31-35 s, or were pure fiction or lists.
- **Real-world loopholes told in 2nd person** did well too: "You win a bucket of balls… the counter won't give you a yen."

## Our structure

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-3 s | **Hook:** a question word (Why/Who/What/When/How), where it fits, immediately followed by the strangest true moment, already on screen. "Why did this captain pour oil into a storm? His ship was about to sink." |
| 3-12 s | **Stakes + doubter:** what's at risk, and who thinks it's crazy |
| 12-40 s | **2-3 escalating steps,** each a visible change, with the threat growing |
| 40-50 s | **Visible payoff:** the moment that proves it |
| 50-60 s | **Mechanism:** 2-3 plain sentences, one everyday analogy, one sourced number |
| 60-65 s | **One-line image ending** + a 3-5-word CTA |

**Channel rule:** start with Why / Who / What / When / How when the story allows it. A bare abstract question with nothing to show in frame 1 is banned. If no question word fits, open on the odd action instead and say so to the user.

**Don't copy:** the "You see," pivot, the exact mocked-inventor template beat for beat, or any of their story subjects. Vary who the doubter is: a rival, a rule, nature, a crowd, a clock.

## Production

- **Scenes:** 12-16, one per line.
- **Animation:** animate 8-16 of them (Shorts are short enough to animate most). With sound effects on at 18 credits/s, a fully animated Short costs about 900-1,200 credits.
- **Truth:** history with dramatised dialogue is fine when the events are documented. Say "the story goes" for legends.
